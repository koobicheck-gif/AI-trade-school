# Professional Trades Academy — Concept Document

> Working name. Alternatives under consideration: **Trades Intelligence Academy**, **Modern Trades Institute**, or an "Academy for the Future Trades Professional" framing. Consolidated from two founding concept memos (2026).

## 1. The thesis

This is not another trade school. It is a **new category**.

Today's trade schools produce **technicians**. The market increasingly needs **professional problem-solvers** — technicians who can:

- communicate with homeowners and create trust
- sell ethically
- use AI and modern field technology
- diagnose faster
- represent the company professionally
- eventually become crew leaders or owners

Trade education is built on three assumptions — learn the technical trade, pass licensing exams, learn business later — while contractors complain about completely different gaps:

> "We can teach HVAC. We can't teach people skills. We can't teach ownership. We can't teach professionalism. We can't teach initiative."

Those are exactly the things GiANT already teaches. The product isn't HVAC. **The product is elite technicians.**

**The promise:** *"We graduate technicians customers trust"* — and for employers, *"We produce technicians that are profitable on Day One."*

**The graduation standard:** not "Can this person repair an HVAC unit?" but:

> "Would I trust this person in my home, representing my company, solving my customer's problem, leading a crew, and eventually running a branch?"

## 2. Competitive landscape (Oklahoma City metro)

Primary competitors:

| Competitor | Position |
|---|---|
| Francis Tuttle Technology Center | Strongest overall; serves Edmond & NW OKC |
| Moore Norman Technology Center | Norman, Moore, south OKC; strong CareerTech programs |
| Metro Tech | Broad technical education across the metro |
| Plumbers & Pipefitters Training Center | Union apprenticeship model |
| ABC of Oklahoma | Merit-shop apprenticeship programs |
| Mid-Del Technology Center | Respected CareerTech provider |

Oklahoma CareerTech is an enormous statewide system — **29 technology centers, 60+ campuses**. Competing head-to-head on traditional vocational education would be extremely difficult.

**Strategy: don't compete — flank.** CareerTech is optimized for *affordable, public, technical, credentialed*. We optimize for *elite, private, employer-driven, leadership-focused, AI-enabled, premium*. Different market, different promise:

- They say: "Learn HVAC."
- We say: **"Become the technician every contractor wants to hire."**

Market signal: CareerTech reportedly has thousands of students waitlisted for skilled-trades seats — demand exceeds capacity. The opening is a differentiated private model, not because public schools are failing, but because the market is larger than existing capacity.

## 3. The Five Intelligences

The curriculum model (originally three, expanded to five):

1. **Technical Intelligence** — how to do the work. HVAC, plumbing, electrical, diagnostics, safety, code, installation, service. Taught by working masters; industry certifications; licensing prep; field labs.
2. **Human Intelligence** — everything GiANT owns. Five Voices, communication, conflict, trust, listening, customer experience/psychology, teamwork, professionalism, leadership, ownership, problem solving, giving/receiving feedback, stress management, sales conversations, professional presence, emotional regulation. *No one owns this space.*
3. **Digital Intelligence** — AI as a diagnostic assistant, job estimation, CRM (ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro), scheduling, photo documentation, voice AI, proposal writing, customer follow-up, video inspections, marketing, automation, business dashboards, prompt engineering, AI troubleshooting. Every graduate leaves with an AI toolkit already in daily use.
4. **Business Intelligence** — where tradespeople become producers. Reading a P&L, gross margin, truck profitability, inventory, marketing, sales, pricing, owning a business. Most tradespeople don't learn this until they're forced into management.
5. **Character Intelligence** — professional formation (not a religious curriculum): reliability, ownership, integrity, discipline, initiative, work ethic, professional image, serving customers, mentoring apprentices.

Human Intelligence is **threaded through the entire program**, not a soft-skills elective: every lab includes communication coaching, every customer simulation includes Five Voices, every internship includes leadership reflection, every student graduates with a Human Intelligence profile.

## 4. Employer-first go-to-market

Most schools recruit students. **We recruit contractors.**

- Begin with ~**20 leading HVAC/plumbing/electrical companies** committed to hiring graduates before the first class starts.
- The employers become the advisory board; every quarter: *"What are your new pain points?"* The curriculum evolves with the market instead of waiting years.
- Parents buy education; **companies buy talent**. The customer is the contractor.

## 5. The Trades Dashboard (the secret weapon)

Every student gets a live performance profile — their "professional operating system" — tracking:

Technical skill · Human Intelligence · Digital Intelligence · leadership · professionalism · customer ratings · AI competency · attendance · coach feedback · internship performance.

Employers don't just receive a diploma — **they receive a complete performance profile**, and can reserve graduates before they hit the market. This is the kernel of the licensable platform.

## 6. Faculty model (teaching-hospital pattern)

Don't hire teachers first. Recruit the best practitioners in the market — the best service manager, field trainer, dispatcher, estimator, operations leader, customer-service leader — many teaching one day every week or two.

- **3–5 full-time coaches** (vs. ~20 full-time instructors in a traditional model)
- **25–40 adjunct industry experts**
- **~100 contractor mentors**

Staffing plan: Executive Director, Operations Director, Admissions, Career Placement; Technical faculty (HVAC master, plumbing master; later electrical, welding, automation); GiANT faculty (communication, leadership, Five Voices, EQ, customer trust); Technology faculty (AI, software, field tech, CRM, automation, digital diagnostics).

## 7. Campus

Looks like a modern service company headquarters, not a high school: HVAC training homes, plumbing homes, smart-home labs, AI command center, dispatch room, customer call-center simulation, sales simulation rooms, truck bays, warehouse, leadership rooms, podcast studio, video production room.

## 8. Curriculum arc (9–12 months, three semesters)

- **Semester 1 — Foundations:** trade fundamentals, professional habits, Five Voices, communication, safety, AI basics, customer service, jobsite excellence.
- **Semester 2 — Application:** advanced diagnostics, leadership, selling with integrity, conflict, using AI, documentation, estimating, problem solving.
- **Semester 3 — Field:** field internship (~240 hrs), apprenticeship pathway, capstone, leadership assessment, career placement.

## 9. Business model — five revenue streams

1. **Student tuition** — $15,000–18,000 for the 9–12 month academy.
2. **Employer partnerships** — companies reserve graduates; placement fees of $2,500–5,000 per hire.
3. **Leadership Academy** — graduates return at years 2, 5, 8: supervisor, foreman, owner training. Powered by GiANT.
4. **Corporate training** — Five Voices, leadership, AI, sales, communication for existing contractor teams (GiANT's current motion, aimed at trades).
5. **Licensing** — license the curriculum/operating system: Tulsa, Dallas, Phoenix, Nashville, Denver, then schools, franchises, and apprenticeship programs nationally.

## 10. Certification & regulatory path

1. **State approval** — Oklahoma private vocational/postsecondary framework approval to legally offer career training.
2. **Industry credentials** — align the program so students earn recognized certifications: EPA 608 refrigerant certification, OSHA safety credentials, other nationally recognized HVAC certifications; plumbing OSHA + apprenticeship/licensing pathways satisfying Oklahoma requirements. Immediate employer credibility.
3. **Proprietary credential** — **Professional Trades Intelligence (PTI) Certification**: Human Intelligence, customer communication, leadership, AI & technology, professionalism, Five Voices. Category creation looks like employers asking applicants: *"Are they PTI Certified?"*

## 11. The moat — where GiANT fits

Anyone can copy HVAC curriculum. No one can easily replicate **twenty-plus years of GiANT intellectual property** around developing people. The relational component is inseparable from the technical component — that's the defensible differentiation, plus the employer network and the dashboard/assessment platform it feeds.

## 12. Scaling — the operating system, not the campuses

This is not a school; it's an **operating system for developing modern trades professionals**. The school is the first delivery mechanism.

- **Phase 1 (Years 1–2):** one flagship academy in Edmond / north OKC. 100–150 graduates annually. Near-100% placement. Founding 20 employer partners.
- **Phase 2 (Years 3–5):** productize the **Professional Trades Operating System** — curriculum, instructor certification, AI platform, assessment tools, Human Intelligence curriculum, leadership curriculum, employer scorecards.
- **Phase 3 (Years 5–10):** license the OS to CareerTech centers, community colleges, private trade schools, major contractors, franchise systems, union and non-union apprenticeship programs. Sell the system rather than owning every campus.

The academy in Edmond or Oklahoma City isn't the end goal — it's the proving ground for a model that could spread nationally.
