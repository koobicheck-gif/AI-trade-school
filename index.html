The problem

Every contractor says the same four things.

Technical training is a solved problem — Oklahoma's CareerTech system alone runs 29 technology centers. What contractors can't hire, and can't teach, is everything else.

"We can teach HVAC."

Technical skill is trainable, testable, and abundant in curriculum form. It's the table stakes, not the differentiator.

"We can't teach people skills."

Communication, trust, conflict, listening — the difference between a callback and a five-star review.

"We can't teach ownership."

Initiative, reliability, follow-through. The traits that turn a tech into a crew leader, and a crew leader into a branch manager.

"We can't teach professionalism."

Showing up, representing the brand, documenting the work, selling with integrity. The stuff customers actually remember.

The category

We don't compete with CareerTech. We flank it.

Public technical education is optimized for affordable, credentialed volume — and demand still exceeds its capacity, with thousands reportedly waitlisted for skilled-trades seats. The white space isn't a cheaper trade school. It's a different promise entirely.

The moat

Anyone can copy an HVAC curriculum. No one can copy twenty years of GiANT.

GiANT's leadership and human-development IP — Five Voices, trust-building, communication, leadership formation — is deployed in thousands of organizations. Here it isn't a "soft skills" elective: it's threaded through every lab, every simulation, every internship reflection. That makes the relational component inseparable from the technical one.

PTI Certification™ — the category-defining credential

Graduates earn the recognized industry credentials (EPA 608, OSHA, licensing pathways) — then add the one nobody else offers: Professional Trades Intelligence Certification, covering human intelligence, customer communication, leadership, AI & technology, and professionalism. Categories are created when employers start asking: "Are they PTI Certified?"

An operating system, not a school

The flagship academy is the proving ground. The durable asset is the Professional Trades Operating System — curriculum, instructor certification, the AI platform, assessment tools, and employer scorecards — licensable to CareerTech centers, community colleges, franchises, and national contractors.

Business model

Five revenue streams, one asset.

Tuition funds the flagship. Employers, alumni, and licensing compound it.

1 · Student tuition

$15,000–18,000 for the 9–12 month academy. 100–150 graduates per year at the flagship.

2 · Employer partnerships

Companies reserve graduates ahead of demand. Placement fees of $2,500–5,000 per hire.

3 · Leadership Academy

Alumni return at years 2, 5, and 8 — supervisor, foreman, and owner training. Powered by GiANT.

4 · Corporate training

Five Voices, leadership, AI, and sales training for existing contractor teams — GiANT's current motion, aimed at the trades.

5 · Licensing

License the operating system — Tulsa, Dallas, Phoenix, Nashville, Denver — then to schools, franchises, and apprenticeship programs nationally.

Why it compounds

Every graduate strengthens the employer network; every employer sharpens the curriculum; every cohort trains the OS that licensing partners buy.
